# RSE v2 — Composition Demonstrator: preregistration

**Status: registration in progress. No model has been trained.** This is a new
experiment under `PROTOCOL_FREEZE.md`'s rule that future work carries its own
registration. Level 1 and Level 2 are closed and are not reopened, reanalysed,
or reinterpreted by anything here.

## Deliverable

One original 45–60 second SNES-style composition, generated by a model trained
on SNES-MDB symbolic performances, rendered through an independently licensed
synthesizer, with reproducible generation and originality checks.

Minimum musical characteristics, to be operationalised in §4 before training:

- a recognisable melodic motif that returns
- rhythm with a discernible pulse
- bass or harmonic accompaniment supporting the melody
- distinct phrases and a recognisable ending
- no clock stagnation and no hardware-inconsistent voice occupancy

## Why this is not Level 2 with a bigger model

Level 2 established that 23.66% better next-token prediction did not yield
coherent music, and that the registered token-grammar criterion passed an
excerpt containing 3,642 simultaneous notes. The diagnosis is that the model
was asked to learn the rules of performance *and* the structure of composition
from an undifferentiated event stream, with constraints that the
representation could have guaranteed outright.

So v2 changes the representation and adds explicit structure. It is a
**hybrid, constrained generative approach** and must be described as such, not
as unconstrained composition.

## 1. Two prerequisite measurements, run before registration

Both on the **train split only**. Artifacts in `~/workplace/v2_artifacts/`.

### 1.1 There is no reliably separable lead voice — step 2 is blocked as stated

`scripts/snes_v2_voice_roles.py`, 200 train tracks, 191 with at least two
pitched voices. Stability is the fraction of 5-second windows in which a
criterion picks the same voice it picks for the whole track.

| criterion | stability, median | stable above 0.8 |
|---|---|---|
| highest median pitch | 0.708 | 43.3% |
| most distinct pitches | 0.384 | 20.0% |
| highest interval rate | 0.556 | 30.0% |

**All three criteria agree on one voice in only 9.9% of tracks.** The best
single rule disagrees with its own track-level choice in about 30% of
5-second windows, consistent with parts migrating across S-DSP voices
mid-track.

Scope of this result: it rules out these three cheap heuristics, not every
possible method. But it rules out the assumption step 2 was reaching for.

**Consequences, registered:**

1. "Train on the melody voice" is **not** an available starting point. A
   hand-labelled lead-voice subset, or a classifier trained on such labels, is
   a **prerequisite task** with its own validation — not an assumption inside
   step 2.
2. The representation must model **musical role separately from hardware
   voice**. The four-token scheme conflated them, and voice index is now
   measured to be an unstable part identity.

### 1.2 Bar length IS measurable per track — but there is no corpus-wide value

`scripts/snes_v2_meter.py`, 196 train tracks. Onsets binned at 10 ms,
normalised autocorrelation, strongest lag sought in 0.3–8.0 s.

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| tracks with strong periodicity (peak ≥ 0.30) | **190 / 196 = 96.9%** |
| median peak autocorrelation | 0.645 |
| autocorrelation at 2× the lag | median 0.520, above 0.2 in 98.4%, never negative |
| median recovered bar length | 1.360 s (p10 0.40, p90 4.23) |
| median pulse (median nonzero inter-onset gap) | 0.1146 s |
| per-track bar/pulse ratio | quartiles **4.6 / 15.3 / 31.9** |

The harmonic result matters: multiples of the lag also peak, in 98.4% of
cases, so this is genuine periodic structure rather than a single-lag
artifact.

**This corrects an earlier objection recorded in this project.** It was
claimed that an A–A′–B–A phrase plan had no measured unit because the release
publishes rows and not bars. The first half is true — there is no time
signature, bar count or rows-per-bar field anywhere in the release, and only
573 of 2,228 tracks carry a software divisor. The conclusion was wrong: bar
length is recoverable from the onsets in 96.9% of tracks, so v2 does **not**
need to restrict itself to the divisor-bearing subset.

**But the ratio does not generalise.** Only 26 of 190 tracks fall within 15%
of 16 rows per bar, 81 are near no tracker value, and the interquartile range
spans 4.6 to 31.9. The median of 15.3 sitting near 16 is a coincidence of
central tendency, not a corpus convention, and reading it as one would be an
error.

**Consequences, registered:**

1. Bar length is **inferred per track** and never hardcoded. The 16-rows-per-
   bar tracker convention is not used as a prior.
2. The inference carries its own validation: a track's bar estimate is
   accepted only if the autocorrelation peak clears 0.30 **and** its first
   harmonic clears 0.20. Tracks failing this are excluded from
   phrase-structured training, and the excluded fraction is reported.
3. An autocorrelation peak is evidence of periodicity, **not proof of a
   notated bar**. The recovered unit may be a half-bar or two-bar period. This
   limitation is stated wherever bar-derived phrase structure is reported.

## 2. Representation (to be fixed before training)

Direction set by the Level 2 failure modes and §1:

- **Simultaneous notes grouped into one time step.** `TIME_SAME` covered 51.9%
  of adjacent attack pairs and both Level 2 runaways were `TIME_SAME` loops.
  The corpus has **zero same-voice retriggers in 1,527,546 ticks**, so a time
  step holds at most eight notes, one per voice. Grouping removes the
  stagnation failure mode **by construction** rather than hoping a model
  avoids it.
- **Explicit voice-occupancy state**, so thousands of overlapping notes are
  unrepresentable rather than merely unlikely.
- **Role modelled separately from hardware voice**, per §1.1.

## 3. Structure

An explicit phrase-level plan (for example A–A′–B–A) with motif recurrence and
variation, the model generating content within it. Phrase boundaries are
placed using the per-track inferred bar length from §1.2, with its validation
and limitations carried through.

## 4. Success criteria

[To be fixed before training. Non-negotiable requirements on this section,
registered now:]

- **Performance-validity predicates alongside any syntactic one.** Level 2's
  grammar criterion passed an excerpt with 3,642 simultaneous notes. Clock
  progress, event-density bounds and voice-lifetime conditions are registered
  conditions, not diagnostics.
- **Perceptual criteria stated as measurable predicates** before generation,
  with the listening protocol and listener count registered in advance.
- **A control for every similarity or structure metric**, computed on real
  held-out music by the identical procedure. This project has twice found a
  metric meaningless without one.

## 5. Rights constraints

- The demonstrator **must not** render through BRR samples extracted from the
  corpus. That ships copyrighted audio regardless of how the notes were
  generated. Synthesis, or independently licensed samples, only.
- The Level 2 neutral renderer is first-party and safe to reuse, though plain.
- Publication of the trained model or generation recipe is subject to the
  unresolved rights review in `RIGHTS.md`.

## 6. Curation disclosure

Human selection of a good generated composition for a public demonstration is
acceptable **and must be disclosed**. What is not acceptable:

- presenting a selected composition as representative of all outputs;
- claiming the model composed parts where musical content was supplied by
  hand.

A demonstration publishes the symbolic output, the audio, the generation
record, and the selection process — how many candidates were generated and on
what basis one was chosen.
