# Listening rubric and scoring rules — FROZEN 2026-11-11

Frozen **before** the second calibration set was generated and before any of
its clips were heard. These are prospective rules for a new instrument and new
observations.

## The three-point scale

| score | label | definition |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | Coherent | Discernible musical phrases **and** pitch relationships that sound intentional |
| **1** | Partially coherent | Some musical organisation is audible, but one criterion is unclear or inconsistent |
| **0** | Incoherent | No convincing phrase organisation, and no pitch relationships that sound intentional |

Judge **structure**, not enjoyment, familiarity, production quality or
stylistic preference. The timbre is deliberately plain in every clip and is
not what is being assessed. A dull but organised passage scores 2; an
interesting but incoherent one scores 0.

## Prospective scoring rules

| rule | value |
|---|---|
| Genuine positive criterion | **≥ 9/10 score 2** |
| Free pitch permutation | **≤ 2/10 score 2** |
| Score 1 | reported separately; **does not** satisfy the positive criterion |
| Paired discrimination | report how many genuine clips score **higher** than their degraded counterpart, ties included |
| Interval-constrained permutation | secondary diagnostic, **no threshold** |

The thresholds are unchanged from the first proposal. What changed is that the
scale now has a defined third category, so no mapping decision is taken after
seeing results.

## Why the first calibration cannot be scored against this gate

The first set offered yes/no and the listener used a third category, *maybe*.
Genuine scored 70% *yes* with 3 *maybe*, which passes the ≥90% criterion if
*maybe* counts as coherent and fails if it does not. **Choosing that mapping
after seeing it flip the outcome is precisely what pre-registration
prevents**, so the first set is left unscored against the primary gate.

What the first set did establish, and what is retained as diagnostic evidence:

- **Sensitivity to destroyed pitch organisation is real.** All 10 tracks
  scored lower in their freely permuted form than genuine: 10/10 paired
  ordering, genuine 0% *no* against permuted 90% *no*.
- **The phrase-order control did not separate** — 7 of 9 shuffled clips rated
  coherent, against 7 of 10 genuine. The honest reading is narrow: *this*
  question did not detect the disruption *this* transformation introduced.
  Rearranging internally coherent phrases can leave each phrase recognisable,
  so listeners may still perceive long-range structure by some other probe.
- **A confound in the free permutation**, raised by the listener: permuting
  the pitch multiset destroys voice-leading and manufactures large register
  leaps, so detection may be driven by unparseable jumps rather than by
  harmonic relationships. Both are musical failures but they are different
  ones. The interval-constrained arm exists to separate them.

## Endpoint status for Milestone 3

| capability | status |
|---|---|
| Melodic and local phrase coherence | **primary endpoint** |
| Preference against the matched G3 baseline | **primary comparison** |
| Continuity across context rollover | secondary, separately evaluated |
| Long-range thematic development | **exploratory only** |
| Intentional motif recall beyond context | not established by this architecture |

Whole-excerpt development is removed from the primary gate. No further
structural control will be built merely to make a long-range criterion pass; a
future study can develop an appropriate probe.

## Standing limitation

A successful calibration by one listener establishes sensitivity for **that
listener, those stimuli and that rendering procedure**. It does not establish
population-level perceptual reliability. The registered main experiment still
requires ≥20 independent listeners and a clustered-bootstrap uncertainty
analysis over listeners and excerpts.
